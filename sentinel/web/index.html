Sentinel

It keeps watching after you sign.

Warden reads a request before your wallet signs it. Sentinel watches the wallet afterwards, around the clock, and tells you in one sentence when something looks wrong: a token account handed to someone else, a payment to a look-alike address, a spending permission you did not give, tokens taken through one you gave long ago.

It watches and it tells you. It cannot stop a thief who empties a wallet in a single transaction, and nobody watching can. It helps when there is a gap: a drain in steps, or a permission taken before the money moves.

Sign in with a wallet

No email and no password. Your wallet signs a short text that names this site, and that is the sign-in. It moves nothing and approves nothing. The wallet you sign in with is the first one Sentinel watches.

I can't find a Solana wallet in this browser. Sentinel signs you in through one: Phantom, Solflare and Backpack all work.

    Where alerts go

    Alerts arrive as a push through ntfy, a free notification app that needs no account. Install it on your phone, subscribe to a topic with a long name nobody could guess, and give Sentinel that topic's address. Whoever knows the topic can read what is sent to it, so treat it like a password. On Pro, alerts can also go to a Discord or Slack channel through a webhook address that service gives you.

      Or have them sent to a Telegram chat. opens Sentinel's bot; press Start there. The bot only delivers alerts: it never sends a link, and never asks you to sign anything or to tell it anything.

      An alert is a plain sentence. It never has a link in it and never asks you to sign anything. A message that does is not from Sentinel.

      These channels are also told when someone signs in to this profile, and before a wallet or a channel is taken out of it, with the wallet that was used. Someone who has stolen one of your keys can sign in here with it, and cannot switch the alerts off without you hearing of it.

          Plan

          Leave

          Removing a wallet makes Sentinel forget what it learned about it and the alerts it raised. Removing the last one removes your profile. The service's own backups keep them for up to seven days more, and then they are gone from those too.

          When an alert is real

          If Sentinel tells you about something you did not do, work out which of two things happened. If you are not sure, treat it as the first.

          Your wallet signed something you did not (a payment you did not make, an account handed to someone else, a sign-in to this page that was not you). Someone else has that wallet's key. A key that has leaked cannot be made safe again, but what is still in the wallet can be moved:

          1. Sign nothing you are sent. After a theft, messages arrive offering to recover the money or "revoke access" through a link. They are the second theft. Sentinel never sends a link and never asks you to sign.
          2. Make a new wallet with a new recovery phrase, on a device you trust. Not another account under the old phrase: the phrase is what leaked.
          3. Move what is left to it, the most valuable first, and SOL last, because every transfer needs a little SOL for its fee. Check the new address against the new wallet itself, every character. Warden will stop and ask, because the old wallet has never paid it.
          4. Then what is locked: a stake or a position in a protocol has to be withdrawn by its own steps before it can be moved.
          5. Tell Sentinel. Add the new wallet here and remove the old one.

          A permission to spend, given or used (a site got an approval to spend one of your tokens; or tokens left in a transaction your wallet did not sign, which is such a permission being used). The key may be fine, and the permission is the danger: whoever holds it can take what it covers whenever they like. Revoke it in your wallet, or move what is left of those tokens to another of your accounts, and do it before anything else.

          If the alert was about something you did, sign in and press "This was me". Sentinel then treats whoever that paid as someone this wallet pays. If it was about something you had listed for sale being sold (a marketplace took it under the permission you gave it when you listed), what that permission takes later is then shown on this page without an alert.
