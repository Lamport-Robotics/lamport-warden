Lamport Warden

Reading lookup tables

Most swaps name their accounts by position in a table stored on chain. To say where the money goes, Warden has to read that table from a Solana RPC. This is the only network call it makes. The RPC learns which table was asked for and your IP address. It does not learn your wallet address, the transaction or the site.

Simulating transactions

Warden reads payments and token transfers exactly. A call to any other program, a swap for example, it can only name. With simulation on, Warden sends such a transaction, unsigned, to the RPC chosen above and asks what it would do, so it can tell you what would leave your wallet and what would arrive. The RPC sees the transaction, and the transaction names your wallet. That is why this is off until you turn it on.

Sites allowed to ask for your Privacy Rings keys

A Privacy Rings wallet derives its keys from one signature. Whoever gets that signature can read your private balance and history for good. Warden blocks the request from every site that is not on this list, and asks you to confirm it on the ones that are.

    What Warden remembers

    For each wallet address, the addresses it has paid and how often. It learns one only when a request you let through comes back signed. It is kept in this browser and sent nowhere.
