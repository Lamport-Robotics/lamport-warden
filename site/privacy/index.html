Privacy

What Warden keeps, and what leaves your browser.

Warden has no account, no Lamport server and no analytics. Lamport Robotics receives nothing from it.

What it keeps, and where

In the browser's extension storage on your machine, and nowhere else:

  • Who each wallet has paid. For each wallet address you sign with: the addresses it has paid, how many times, the amounts in SOL, and when. Warden uses this to tell a familiar payment from a new one and to spot look-alike addresses. It records a payment only when a request you let through comes back signed.
  • Your settings. The RPC address, whether simulation is on, and the sites you allowed to ask for Privacy Rings keys.
  • Wallet objects it could not guard. Their names, with the day each was last seen.

The settings page shows all of it and can forget a wallet's history. Removing the extension removes it all.

Warden does not keep the sites you visit or the requests it read.

What leaves your browser

Two kinds of request, both to the Solana RPC chosen on the settings page. By default that is the public RPC at api.mainnet-beta.solana.com. It can be your own, or turned off.

  1. Reading lookup tables. When a transaction names its accounts through a table stored on chain, Warden asks the RPC for that table. The RPC receives the table's address and, like any server, your IP address. It does not receive your wallet address, the transaction or the site.
  2. Simulation, only if you turn it on. For a transaction that calls a program Warden cannot read, Warden sends the unsigned transaction and the list of accounts it writes to the RPC, which runs it and answers with the result. The transaction names your wallet. This is off until you turn it on.

Requests are sent without cookies and without a referrer. Nothing is sent to anyone else, nothing is sold, and nothing is used for advertising or to decide creditworthiness or lending.

What it can see on a page

Warden runs on every https page because any page can ask a wallet to sign. There it does two things: it puts itself in front of the wallet's signing calls, and it draws its prompt. It does not read the page's content, your passwords or your keys. It never has your private keys: those stay in your wallet.
