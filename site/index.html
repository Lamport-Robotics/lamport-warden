Lamport Warden

It reads the request before you sign it.

Warden is a browser extension that sits in front of the Solana wallet you already use. Before a signing request reaches the wallet, Warden says in one sentence what it will do to your money, and stops it when that is not what you meant.

How it answers

Four answers.
Most of the time you see none of them.

Warden keeps its own record of who you have paid. It learns an address only when a request you let through comes back signed, so dust sent to you by a look-alike teaches it nothing.

Allow

Nothing appears. An address and a size you have confirmed before.

Explain

One sentence, with Cancel and Continue. A first payment to a new address.

Confirm

The sentence, and you type a short confirmation before it goes on.

Block

The sentence and the reason. There is no button to continue.

What it stops

The rules the robot runs.
In your browser.

They are the same rules as on Pincer1, tested against a replay of 14 known drain patterns, all of which are stopped.

  • Look-alike addressesThe same first and last four characters as an address you have paid, but a different address.
  • Account handoverA hidden instruction that reassigns your account, or a token account's authority, to someone else.
  • Unlimited approvalsA delegate allowed to move all of a token, now or later.
  • Sign now, sent laterA durable-nonce transaction that stays valid long after you have forgotten it.
  • A transaction dressed as a messageOr a raw hash. Warden lets a message through only when you can read all of it.
  • The signature that unlocks private keysA site asking for the one signature a Privacy Rings wallet derives its keys from.

Sentinel keeps watching after you sign.

Warden reads a request before your wallet signs it. Sentinel is the other half: a service that watches the wallet afterwards, around the clock, and tells you in one sentence when something looks wrong.

You sign in with a wallet, not an email. Watching three wallets is free.

Open Sentinel

What it can and cannot do.

It tells you when a token account is handed to someone else, a payment goes to a look-alike address, a spending permission appears that you did not give, or tokens are taken through one you gave long ago. An alert is a plain sentence with no link in it.

It cannot stop a thief who empties a wallet in a single transaction, and nobody watching can.

Watch is free.

  • 3 wallets watched around the clock, each read every minute
  • Alerts in plain sentences, pushed to your phone through ntfy
  • "This was me", so it learns who you pay

What leaves your browser.

There is no account and no model, and by default nothing goes to Lamport. Your history stays in the extension's storage on your machine.

By default Warden makes one kind of network call. Most swaps name their accounts by position in a table stored on chain, and to say where the money goes Warden reads that table from a Solana RPC. The RPC learns which table was asked for and your IP address, not your wallet, the transaction or the site. You can point it at your own RPC, or turn it off.

You can also turn on simulation. Warden then sends a swap, unsigned, to your RPC and tells you what would leave your wallet and what would arrive. The RPC sees that transaction, so it stays off until you choose it.

If you use Sentinel, you can let Warden tell it which signatures were yours, so it stays quiet about your own payments. That sends a wallet's address and a transaction's signature, and nothing else. It is off until you turn it on.

Advice, not a lock.

Warden is a second pair of eyes in front of your wallet. A page can still reach a wallet in ways an extension never sees, and turning Warden off removes it.

On Pincer1 the same rules are enforced by the robot's controller and shown on its own screen, where a compromised computer cannot talk them away.
